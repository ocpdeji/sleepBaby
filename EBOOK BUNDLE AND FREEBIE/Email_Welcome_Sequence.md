## EMAIL 2 — Sent 24 Hours Later

**Subject line options:**
- "The 3-minute pause that changes everything"
- "What I do when my baby cries at 2am"
- "One thing for tonight"

**Body:**

If you've already used the cheat sheet — incredible. If you haven't — that's also fine. You're surviving. That's the whole job right now.

Today I want to give you ONE quick win. Something you can do tonight, no setup, no equipment.

It's called **The 3-Minute Pause.**

Here's what it is: when your baby makes a sound at night, before you do anything — wait 3 minutes.

Set a timer if you have to. Listen carefully. Are they actually crying, or are they just rustling and making sleep noises?

Most parents go in within 30 seconds because we're terrified of letting our babies cry. But in those 30 seconds, we accidentally **wake the baby up** who was actually halfway through cycling through a light sleep phase.

The 3-minute pause gives them a chance to resettle on their own — which is the entire skill we're trying to teach.

Try it tonight. Just for one waking. See what happens.

Within 3 days, you'll start hearing the difference between your baby's "I'm cycling" sounds and their "I genuinely need you" cry. That single skill is worth its weight in gold.

I'll write again in a couple days.

Sleep is coming.

— Six & Thriving

P.S. If your baby is genuinely distressed (escalating cry, signs of pain, fever) — go in. Always. The pause is for the rustles, not the screams.

The first night is hard. The second night might be a little better. The third night is **a disaster.**

This is called the **extinction burst.** It's a known psychological phenomenon: when a previously rewarded behavior (your baby crying until they're picked up) stops being rewarded, the behavior **escalates dramatically before it disappears.**

It's the toddler tantrum when the iPad is taken away. It's the addict's withdrawal symptom right before they break free. It's the system frantically trying to make the old solution work one more time.

And in the moment, it feels exactly like failure.

This is the night most parents quit.

It's also the night that, if you hold the line, almost always leads to a dramatically better Night 4.

## After Email 5: Ongoing Newsletter

After Email 5 (whether they bought or not), move them to your weekly newsletter.

**Cadence:** 1 email per week. Tuesday or Thursday morning.

**Content rotation:**
- Week 1: Quick win / parent tip
- Week 2: Personal story
- Week 3: Q&A / mythbust
- Week 4: Soft pitch (book, bundle, freebie of the month)

Then repeat. Never skip a week. Never make it pure sales. Always lead with usefulness.

## Pro Tips for Email Marketing

1. **Use a real "from" name.** "Six & Thriving" — not "Six & Thriving Newsletter"
2. **No images in welcome emails.** Plain text feels personal. Visuals feel corporate.
3. **One CTA per email.** Don't ask people to do 3 things.
4. **Reply to every reply.** Especially in the first 90 days. This is how you get superfans.
5. **Track open rate, not just click rate.** Open >40% = great. Open <20% = subject line problem.
6. **Segment by behavior.** "Bought eBook" gets different emails than "still on free list."
